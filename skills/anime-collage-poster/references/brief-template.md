# The 18-paragraph collage brief

The working briefs (`assets/briefs/*.txt`) all share this structure. It descends from the original reference pack's brief, which was about 1900 words. To make a new poster, copy the closest example and edit it paragraph by paragraph. Keep the sentence patterns, because they are what made Krea2 produce poster structure.

Throughout, "the character" means the subject's name. Replace it in every paragraph; it appears roughly 14 times.

| # | Paragraph | What it controls | What to change for a new character or theme |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Format and subject | Who is in the poster, how she looks, the pose, and where she sits on the canvas | The whole identity block (hair, eyes, accessories, outfit), the must-have props and actions, and the attitude. "Vertical 4:5" can stay, because the latent size (1920×1080) sets the real aspect |
| 2 | Composition | Asymmetry, negative space in the upper-left and lower-right, conflicting axes (lower-left → upper-right diagonal, vertical stack behind the subject, horizontal strip), bleed off the edges | Usually only the name |
| 3 | Palette | 10–14 named colors; which dominate the background and overlays; what white and black are used for; where the accent blocks go | Swap for the theme pack. This is the strongest mood lever |
| 4 | Background surfaces | Matte paper, tracing paper, photocopy, painted cardboard, graph paper, notebook edges, fabric grain, plastic sheets, film frames; "not a realistic room" | The cardboard color plus theme surfaces such as lacquer, brocade, paper-cut lattices, mirror-ball, lasers or a pixel mosaic |
| 5 | Character echoes | 3–5 misregistered copies, their tints and crops | Name the crops after the character's signature parts (drills and shoulder; buns and collar; a bob and hoodie sleeve) |
| 6 | Cut-out masks on the body | Paper masks and torn strips over the sleeves and outer hair; inverse-color masks | Mask colors, plus everything that must stay uncovered: every face (hers and any companion's) and any must-have detail, such as an opened stomach panel |
| 7 | Prop cluster (lower-left) | 10–15 cut-paper objects built around one signature prop | The signature prop and the object list from the theme pack, plus the cluster colors |
| 8 | Geometric blocks | Big blocks behind the head, a torn semicircle at the upper-left, an accent strip at the middle-right, fragments at the lower-left, black silhouettes at the edges | Block colors, plus an optional hero element (e.g. a dragon cut-out winding from lower-left to upper-right) |
| 9 | Hollow typography | Exactly three readable strings: an oversized thin hollow title cropped vertically along the left, a narrow outlined uppercase brand across a strip at the upper or middle right, and a small word near the prop cluster | The three strings. Keep them short |
| 10 | Micro labels | Tiny multilingual labels as accents | A few theme words. Each extra label is another chance for garbling |
| 11 | Identity mark | A small logo-like emblem plus a compact outlined label in a corner, registration crosses and crop marks | The label text |
| 12 | Tape | Three strips: behind the head, pinning the prop cluster, holding a block at the upper right | Tape colors and what the strips hold |
| 13 | Ink marks | Contours, loops, scribbles and colored technical lines, kept abstract | Line colors, plus a mood adjective ("emotionally unstable" vs "playful") |
| 14 | Halftone and print | Halftone in panels, fine dots around the hair, photocopy grain in the echoes, misregistration around the title; the focal area kept clean | Dot colors, and what must stay clean (face, prop, companion) |
| 15 | Lighting | The front key light that the user picked for Teto | Hair-sheen wording; catchlights, or eyelid highlights if her eyes are closed; outfit and prop highlights; rim-light color; brightness |
| 16 | Hierarchy | Face and upper torso → prop cluster and the signature shape → typography, echoes and blocks → small details | Add any must-have item (e.g. a bubble) to level 1 |
| 17 | Order vs disorder | The character stays stable at the center while the collage behaves like fragmented memory; fragments drift off the edges | The attitude word ("emotionally stable", "cocky and full of energy"), plus theme motifs ("clock hands spinning loose", "steam curling", "the dragon twisting") |
| 18 | Final atmosphere | A keyword list | Palette and material words, theme words, mood ("bright pop" vs "glossy purple-black Chinese pop"), and "a … <name> focal point" |

## Adaptation checklist

1. Replace the name everywhere, then search for the old name. It should appear zero times.
2. Rewrite paragraph 1 from a confirmed identity (`anime-illustration/references/characters.md` in the sibling skill folder). Put must-have props and actions in concrete terms, with size and position.
3. Swap palette words consistently across paragraphs 3, 4, 8, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 18.
4. Swap the signature prop consistently across paragraphs 1, 6, 7, 12, 14, 15 and 16.
5. Set the texts in 9, 10 and 11, and keep them few and short.
6. Set the attitude in 1 and 17. For "youthful, not dark or mature", add "keep her bright and youthful" to 1 and keep the lighting bright in 15.
7. Read the whole brief once for leftovers from the previous theme, e.g. "bakery collage", "crimson", "Teto".
8. Expect about 2100–2600 words. The bundled briefs run 2117 (Teto), 2358 (hoodie) and 2599 (China) words; the original pack brief is about 1900.

## Hard constraints the model ignores

Constraints such as "no long hair below the buns", "only one companion" or "the bubble must be visible" are often ignored even when stated twice. Put them in paragraph 1, render three seeds, and pick. If the user loves an image that breaks one, fix it locally or accept it. The user accepted long tails under the buns.
